# Reply card: the rules the meter enforces
Write the answer first. Add the reason second. Add the caveat last.
Keep every sentence under 25 words. Split at the conjunction instead of running on.
Use the imperative mood in instructions. Replace should, must and might with a direct command.
Spell contractions out in full: do not, cannot, it is, we will.
Use a full stop where a semicolon would go. Two sentences beat one hinge.
Write for example and that is, never e.g. or i.e.
Cut slop words: leverage, robust, seamless, delve, unlock, elevate, streamline, holistic, crucial, myriad.
Name the actor at the start of the sentence. Do not open with an -ing clause.
Pick one verb for one action. Do not rotate check, verify, confirm and validate.
Use a comma, a colon or a full stop. Never an em dash and never a spaced double hyphen.
Never use an exclamation mark. The code already raised its voice.
Do not close with In summary, In conclusion or To sum up. Stop when the answer stops.
Put one claim per line. A fault points at one line, not a paragraph.
Quote paths, ports, flags and error strings exactly as they appear.
Number ordered steps one per line. Prose is not a sequence diagram.
Say what you verified and how. Do not imply a test you did not run.
Prefer the concrete noun: the file, the port, the function. Not the resource, the surface, the layer.
Target zero faults per 100 words. One em dash is still a fault.
Apply this card to technical replies only. Creative writing is exempt.
